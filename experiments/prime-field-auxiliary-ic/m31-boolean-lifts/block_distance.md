# Approximation by low-degree Boolean polynomials

The target label is whether x³+2x+14 is a square modulo 2147483647, including zero. The degree is the total degree in the 31 input bits in algebraic normal form over GF(2). Fixing 27 high bits leaves a polynomial of at most the original degree on the four low bits. For every one of the 134217727 full 16-coordinate blocks, we allow a separate best polynomial of the specified degree. Their summed errors lower-bound the errors of any single polynomial of that degree. Omitting the remaining 15 coordinates only weakens the bound. The exact error of a single constant follows more directly from the full positive count.

Compile block_distance.c with clang -O3 -std=c11 and run it to regenerate block_distance.csv. The field scan uses a square-residue bitmap and checks the full positive count against 1073752646. The distance transform starts from all truth tables generated by valid square-free monomials. A separate exhaustive check extracts the ANF of every four-bit truth table to verify the zero set, and checks every nonzero distance against the minimum over all neighbors. Hence every bit flip can decrease the distance by at most one. BlockDistance.lean proves that a path to any zero then has length at least the starting distance. The native program evaluates the numerical tables and counts; the Lean theorem covers the graph implication.
