# An elementary missing-direction argument for 17 seven-bit addresses

Let S contain 17 distinct seven-bit addresses. Suppose their unordered pairwise XORs cover all 127 nonzero seven-bit words. There are 136 unordered pairs. Remove one occurrence of each of the 127 words from their multiset of differences. Exactly nine occurrences remain, with repetitions allowed. Call them d₁ through d₉.

For a seven-bit parity mask u, form the nine-bit signature f(u) whose jth bit is the binary inner product of u with dⱼ. This map is linear in u. Fix a nonzero u. Exactly 64 of the seven-bit words have odd inner product with it, since flipping any bit selected by u pairs odd and even words. Let w be the number of keys in S whose inner product with u is odd. Exactly w(17−w) unordered pairs of keys cross to the opposite parity. Counting the same pairs by difference gives 64 plus the Hamming weight of f(u). This weight is nonnegative, and evaluating w from 0 to 17 gives precisely the possibilities 2, 6, and 8.

Every nonzero input therefore has nonzero signature. By linearity f is injective and has 128 outputs. The possible outputs consist of zero and words of weight 2, 6 or 8, a total of 1 + 36 + 84 + 9 = 130. Four of the weight-two words have pairwise disjoint supports, for example the integers 3, 12, 48 and 192. A linear image can contain at most one of these four: the XOR of any two has forbidden weight four. Thus the image contains at most 127 words. This contradiction establishes a missing nonzero direction.
