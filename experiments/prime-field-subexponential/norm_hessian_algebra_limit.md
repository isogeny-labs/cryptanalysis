# The split norm Hessians generate the full matrix algebra

The signed-fiber norm inverse is a system of quadratic coefficient equations
in the affine section parameters.  Polynomial convolution makes each Hessian
structured.  A possible compressed inverse would simultaneously diagonalize
these Hessians, or put them into common blocks of bounded size, and solve the
resulting scalar or small-block systems.

The exact split panels rule out that mechanism.  For every section dimension
\(r=2,\ldots,7\), the unital associative algebra generated by the coefficient
Hessians is

\[
 M_r(\mathbf F_{101}).
\]

The closure reaches dimension \(r^2\) after one multiplication round for
\(r=2,\ldots,6\) and after two rounds for \(r=7\).  Respectively 5/6, 9/10,
14/15, 18/21, 25/28, and 30/36 Hessian pairs fail to commute.  Since the
generated algebra is the full matrix algebra, it remains full after scalar
extension and has no nontrivial common invariant subspace.  Therefore the
quadrics admit neither simultaneous diagonalization nor a common bounded
block decomposition in these panels.

This does not remove convolutional structure from the individual matrices.
Toeplitz/Hankel multiplication can still accelerate an iteration, and a
nonlinear inverse could use relations among the whole coefficient sequence.
The result only closes the direct spectral route in which all norm quadrics
share eigenvectors or small invariant blocks.  It is a finite exact control,
not an asymptotic circuit lower bound or an exponent improvement.
