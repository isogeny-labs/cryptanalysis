# Lefschetz structure of the norm-convolution quotient

The socle census shows substantial parity structure, but does not determine
whether multiplication through the quotient is otherwise degenerate. This
control tests powers of a fixed linear form

\[
  \ell=c_1z_1+\cdots+c_rz_r
\]

between every pair of graded components of the deterministic norm quotient.
A form is weak Lefschetz when multiplication by $\ell$ has maximum rank in
each adjacent degree, and strong Lefschetz when every power $\ell^k$ has
maximum rank between every degree pair.

All 112 seeded random forms are weak Lefschetz over $\mathbb F_{101}$, and 111
are strong Lefschetz. The sole exception is a dimension-three form whose
adjacent maps have maximum rank but whose square vanishes. Dimensions four
through nine have 16/16 strong forms. The sparse all-ones form is weak
Lefschetz through dimension six, then fails by ranks 17/19, 25/26, and 33/34
in the first affected maps for dimensions seven, eight, and nine. Thus the
failures lie on special coefficient loci rather than reflecting a generic
multiplication obstruction in these controls.

Strong Lefschetz structure does give a one-directional Jordan-chain
decomposition. It does not make that decomposition small: the number of
chains is the peak Hilbert coefficient, namely 3, 4, 8, 13, 21, 40, and 66 in
dimensions three through nine. Those peaks follow the exponential middle
layer already identified by the Hilbert control.

## Exact limit

This is a finite-field calculation for 16 forms per dimension and one
deterministic norm coefficient triple. It is not a proof of strong Lefschetz
for generic norm ideals. Even a general theorem would only simplify powers of
one multiplication operator; it would not simultaneously compress the other
coordinate multiplications or solve the affine norm system. No DLP exponent
improvement follows from this control.
