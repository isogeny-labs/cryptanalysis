# Odd-degree hyperelliptic norm equations have no Pell-unit infrastructure

Let

\[
 C:y^2=f(x),\qquad \deg f=2g+1,
\]

over a finite field of odd characteristic, and let

\[
 A=\mathbf F_p[x,y]/(y^2-f)

\]

be its affine coordinate ring.  The smooth projective curve has exactly one
geometric point \(P_\infty\) outside \(\operatorname{Spec}A\).

If \(\epsilon\in A^\times\), then the divisor of \(\epsilon\) is supported
at \(P_\infty\).  A principal divisor has degree zero, so its coefficient at
that single point is zero.  Hence \(\operatorname{div}(\epsilon)=0\), and a
global regular function on the projective curve is constant.  Therefore

\[
 A^\times=\mathbf F_p^\times,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}A^\times=0.
\]

The norm-one affine units are only the constants satisfying \(c^2=1\), hence
\(c=\pm1\).  In particular, a solution of

\[
 R^2-fB^2=N

\]

does not sit in an orbit of a positive-rank Pell unit group.  Polynomial
continued fractions can test or reconstruct a selected principal ideal, but
there is no regulator direction that walks through the split-prime
orientations in this imaginary model.

Hilbert 90 does not change this conclusion.  A nonconstant norm-one element
has the form \(\gamma/\bar\gamma\), but it necessarily has poles.  Its divisor
is

\[
 D-\bar D,

\]

so choosing its poles chooses ideal classes and reintroduces the orientation
equation.  Hilbert 90 parametrizes the fiber after a rational denominator is
specified; it does not select the denominator or a principal split-prime
allocation.

The ledger records unit rank zero in genera two through seven.  The argument
holds in every genus for odd-degree models.  It does not cover a real
even-degree model with two rational points at infinity, whose affine unit
rank can be one; nor does it rule out a global algorithm using ideals without
claiming they are generated by a Pell-unit orbit.  For the current binary
odd-degree construction, however, continued-fraction infrastructure is not
the missing subexponential mechanism.
