# Bounded-denominator public lifts recover a larger relation family

The frozen rational-`x` search found 44 rational point lifts and 212 short
rational relations across the same 3,318 public subgroup points as the
integer-`x` control. It charged **8,286,306** inspected `(denominator,
shift)` candidates, including every failed gcd and square test. The
independent Sage replay checked all candidate outcomes and all 2,968
rational group tests.

For each reduced public point `R=(x,y)` and each `1≤d≤25`, the rule sets
`a₀` to the centered residue of `x·d² mod p`, then inspects
`a=a₀+kp` for `k=0,−1,+1,...,−50,+50`. It accepts the first coprime
`(a,d)` satisfying the exact square equation

`b² = a³ − a d⁴ − 2d⁶`,

with `b/d³` reducing to the public `y`. Thus `(a/d²,b/d³)` lies on the
fixed rational curve. The rule uses public point coordinates and the
frozen search order; `Q=17G` is a fixture whose scalar is used only for
replay. The 3,266 nonidentity point-search failures and eight identities
remain in the raw shards, with each row's probe count and chosen rational
representation.

Denominator one recovers `±P`; denominator four adds `±2P`; denominator
25 adds `±3P` in the seven fields where `P` generates the selected
subgroup. The remaining field's prime subgroup is generated by `2P`, so
only `±2P` are admitted by this bound. Every ordered pair of distinct
lifted points has one relation in the frozen 14-vector primitive family.
All 212 relation rows have nonzero coefficient sum modulo their subgroup
order and independently replay scalar 17. The structural reduction and
scalar-recovery implications are the previously checked Lean declarations
`XedniDirectRelation.finite_group_identity` and
`XedniDirectRelation.finite_relation_recovers`.

## Cost comparison and boundaries

The parent integer-`x` window found 14 point lifts and 14 rational
relations with 69,230 candidate probes. This denominator arm gives about
3.14 times as many point lifts and 15.14 times as many relation rows for
about 119.69 times as many candidate probes. These are exact finite-panel
counts for different search spaces, not matched wall times or an IC
speedup. Every rational relation reduces to a public-group relation for
its known coefficient vector; the saved cost does not establish an
advantage over testing those vectors directly.

Each point lift was constructed once and reused for ordered pairs. The
complete two-offset schedule contains 1,471,112 pair queries. Repeating
both point searches independently on every pair would require a **derived**
7,352,043,076 candidate probes under the saved per-point costs; that
schedule was not executed. The stated 8,286,306 probes are the actual
point-wise precomputation charge.

`protocol.json` froze the parent source and result hashes, eight fields,
denominator and shift bounds, first-success rule, query law, and coefficient
family. The pure-Python producer retained deterministic gzip JSONL rows.
The repository's checked Sage launcher saved its runtime receipt and
replayed every candidate, accepted rational point, reduction, rational
relation, and scalar certificate. The audit and producer exited zero with
empty stderr. `verify.py` and `SEAL.sha256` check the source, input,
records, and result identities.

The next falsifier is a lift construction that changes the rational curve
or chooses it jointly for a pair, with its candidate work charged before
the relation test. A larger denominator window on this same rank-one
curve can reveal more multiples, but its point-wise yield and coefficient
search must be compared on the same query law.
