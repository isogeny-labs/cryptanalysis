# Indexed lift containment and a two-point rational relation

An indexed family of compatible rational lifts can change the curve, lifted points, query, and integer coefficient vector at every attempt. The checked implication is that **each rational relation maps to a public relation with the same vector**. The precommitted two-point search tested 26,624 candidate constructions and found one exact rational relation. It occurred at candidate 16,179 in the fixed order, on the p = 251, q = 61 instance.

## Construction and result

The [protocol](protocol.json) fixes eight prime fields, four SHA-derived target fixtures per field, sixteen ordered distinct public query pairs per target, thirteen selected integer vectors, and four vector-dependent coordinate shifts. For every attempt, the runner lifts both public points to integer coordinates, determines rational A and B from their two short-Weierstrass equations, checks nonsingularity and integral good reduction to `y² = x³ − x − 2`, and evaluates the proposed rational relation. It computes the finite-field relation diagnostic after the rational test. The construction does not filter candidates by that diagnostic.

The 508 attempts with a public relation comprise 127 query-vector events under the four shifts. One yielded the rational relation; the other 507 yielded rational nonrelations. The separate eight positive and eight negative rational controls all matched their expected statuses. All attempted rows, including rejects, are retained in [rows.jsonl](rows.jsonl); [result.json](result.json) contains the counts and source digests. The raw rows have SHA-256 `3c75b8f6aed964b99e3135cda848289669f7f6bf54a7a8c603ec37b9198e5b9e`.

The hit uses `p = 251`, subgroup order `q = 61`, target index 3, query offsets `(2,3)`, and vector `(2,−1)`. Its rational curve and points are

```text
E/Q: y² = x³ + 501x + 15811
P = (17, −171), R = (−18, 31), 2P = R
discriminant = −116042791536
```

Reduction gives `U = (17,80)` and `V = (233,31)` on the specified curve over `F_251`, with `2U = V`. The target is `Q = (17,171)` and the query points are `U = Q − 2G`, `V = Q − 3G`; hence `2(Q − 2G) − (Q − 3G) = 0`, which yields `Q = G` in the order-61 subgroup. Scalar 1 was independently replayed against the frozen target fixture. [hit.json](hit.json) and [hit-sage-audit.json](hit-sage-audit.json) retain the exact certificate. The fixture scalar was generated by the frozen SHA law; it is recorded for independent replay and was not used to select the rational curve.

## Proof and verification map

The mathematical proof is homomorphic: for each candidate `i`, apply its reduction homomorphism to `aᵢ Pᵢ + bᵢ Rᵢ = 0`. Compatibility of the two point reductions gives `aᵢ Uᵢ + bᵢ Vᵢ = 0`. Thus changing rational models, shifts, or coefficient vectors within an indexed family cannot turn a finite-field nonrelation for that same candidate into a compatible rational relation.

| Result | Lean declaration | Exact scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indexed family with common public points | `indexed_lift_relation_reduces` | Arbitrary index type, additive commutative groups, homomorphic reductions, integer vectors, and two point-matching hypotheses |
| Indexed family with varying public queries | `indexed_query_lift_relation_reduces` | Same hypotheses, with public `Uᵢ,Vᵢ` indexed by candidate |
| Nonrelation corollary | `indexed_lift_no_public_relation` | Every public candidate vector is a nonrelation |
| Fixed-vector search | `fixed_vector_lift_search_containment` | Arbitrarily many lifts/models for one `U,V,a,b` |

All four declarations are in [LiftFamilyContainment.lean](formal/LiftFamilyContainment.lean). The pinned toolchain is Lean 4 `v4.35.0-rc4`, with Mathlib revision `021c46bdd042f67b538b9f9871d556a93713a9a9`. `lake build LiftFamilyContainment` completed; the [build output](formal/build-output.txt) records `#print axioms` as `[propext]` for each theorem. The group-theoretic implication is fully checked. Formal definitions connecting every concrete elliptic curve's good-reduction map to these group homomorphisms remain an open bridge; the concrete instance is checked by independent arithmetic audits rather than asserted as a Lean elliptic-curve theorem.

The full [independent Python audit](audit.py) reconstructed all 26,624 candidate curves, statuses, public relations, controls, and the scalar replay using separate finite and rational group laws; [audit.json](audit.json) records `PASS`. A second check through the repository's checked Sage launcher recomputed the hit's curve, reductions, relation, and scalar replay; [hit-sage-audit.json](hit-sage-audit.json) records `PASS`. The checked Sage runtime receipt is [sage-runtime.json](sage-runtime.json). This is a bounded correctness and candidate-count experiment on the local host, with a 900-second cap and no isolated wall-time comparison.

The next experiment is a preregistered larger-field and wider-shift panel to measure how often this exact two-point construction lifts public relations, retaining every attempted shift and both control classes. A mathematical follow-up is to characterize the Diophantine condition on two lifted points that produces `2P = R` under good reduction.
