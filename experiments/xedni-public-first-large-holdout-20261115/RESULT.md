## Construction and accounting

The fixed curve is `Y²=X³−X−2`. For each field, `G` is the pinned subgroup multiple of the reduction of `(2,2)` and `Q=17G`. The known scalar constructs the fixture outside the public test stage. At query `(t₁,t₂)`, the producer forms `U=Q−t₁G` and `V=Q−t₂G`, tests all 13 vectors with finite-curve group operations, and constructs a rational model only for a public zero relation. For a hit `(a,b)`, it computes `T=[b⁻¹]U`, centers its coordinates `(x,y)`, and sets `B=y²−x³+x`. On `E_B/ℚ`, the points `[b](x,y)` and `[−a](x,y)` satisfy the rational relation and reduce to `U,V`.

The construction stage performed 142 modular inverses, 142 public scalar multiplications, 142 rational curve constructions, 284 rational scalar multiplications, 142 rational relation tests, 284 reduction checks, and 142 scalar replays. These are stage-operation counts. The deterministic query census does not estimate a random-input rate or a wall-time speedup.

## Direct scalar recovery from a public hit

The checked Lean declaration `public_relation_iff_recovered_scalar` proves that if `a+b≠0`, then `a(w−t₁)+b(w−t₂)=0` is equivalent to `w=(a t₁+b t₂)/(a+b)` in the scalar field. `public_relation_target_unique` proves that the same public relation and offsets admit only one target scalar. The finite-curve producer had already verified each public group relation before constructing a rational model; this scalar formula therefore recovers the target from a hit without using the later rational model. The formal statement is at the scalar-field level; the existing finite-group checks and independent Sage audit supply the concrete group-to-scalar replay for this panel.

`public_recovery.py` used only the saved hit vector, offsets, and subgroup order to apply that formula to all **142** events, then compared each result with the fixture. `public_recovery_audit.py` independently enumerated every scalar residue for each event and found exactly one satisfying the relation, equal to the saved scalar in all 142 cases. The two frozen post-analysis protocols pin the parent result, sources, and Lean theorem before their respective runs. The replay does not charge these post-analysis checks to the producer's original timing. `lake build PublicRecovery` completed successfully (9,046 jobs); both declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`.

The versioned `formal/PublicGroupRecovery.lean` proves the structural group bridge. Given a field `F`, an additive `F`-module representing the public subgroup, and an **injective linear** scalar-to-point map `φ`, it proves `a • φ(w−t₁)+b • φ(w−t₂)=0` if and only if `w=(a t₁+b t₂)/(a+b)` when `a+b≠0`. Thus the direct scalar recovery statement holds at the group-operation level under exactly the coordinate-map property required by the finite-curve application. The concrete elliptic-curve subgroup map and reduction compatibility remain formal proof obligations; the Sage producer and audit checked the concrete panel instances. `lake build PublicGroupRecovery` completed successfully (9,047 jobs), and `#print axioms` reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. This new module leaves the earlier hash-pinned `PublicRecovery.lean` and its build receipt byte-identical.

`formal/PrimeOrderRecovery.lean` removes the separately assumed injective coordinate map for a prime-order scalar module. For any nonzero public point `G` in a `ZMod q` module with prime `q`, it proves that `a • ((w−t₁) • G)+b • ((w−t₂) • G)=0` is equivalent to the same direct recovery formula when `a+b≠0`. Its torsion specialization constructs the `ZMod q` action when every point in the chosen additive group is killed by `q`. The new `cyclic_torsion` theorem derives that condition for the subgroup generated by `G` solely from `q • G=0`; `cyclic_public_relation_iff_recovered_scalar` then proves direct scalar recovery within that subgroup. `lake build PrimeOrderRecovery` completed successfully (9,048 jobs); all four theorems report only standard Lean foundations under `#print axioms` (`cyclic_torsion` uses `propext` alone).

`formal/ExceptionalRelation.lean` closes the zero-sum coefficient case. If `a+b=0`, the scalar relation is equivalent to `a(t₂−t₁)=0`; for `a≠0`, this is exactly `t₁=t₂`. The same equivalence holds for point-group operations in a prime-order scalar module with nonzero generator. Thus a zero-sum hit on two distinct offsets is impossible within these assumptions, while equal offsets give a relation independent of the target scalar. `lake build ExceptionalRelation` completed successfully (9,049 jobs), and all three new declarations report only standard Lean foundations under `#print axioms`. The panel's selected vectors all have nonzero sum, so this theorem classifies a boundary case rather than changing its 142 recorded hits.

`formal/DistinctOffsetRecovery.lean` combines both coefficient cases into an exact public certificate theorem. Given distinct offsets `t₁≠t₂`, nonzero `a`, nonzero generator `G`, and prime `q`, a public point-group relation implies `a+b≠0` and `w=(a t₁+b t₂)/(a+b)`. Its converse proves that these two scalar conditions imply the public relation. Thus a valid distinct-offset hit already contains the target scalar in its publicly known coefficients and offsets. `lake build DistinctOffsetRecovery` completed successfully (9,050 jobs); both declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`. The theorem is conditional on finding the hit; it does not bound the work of finding one adaptively.

`formal/ConcreteCurveRecovery.lean` instantiates the certificate theorem on Mathlib's actual affine elliptic-curve point group. It defines the exact reduced curve `y²=x³−x−2`, proves discriminant `−1664` and ellipticity when prime `p` does not divide `1664`, constructs the affine point `(2,2)`, and proves that point is nonzero. The final theorem applies distinct-offset recovery to the subgroup it generates, with the exact remaining hypothesis `q • (2,2)=0` for prime subgroup order `q`. Seven checked Sage panel rows use this raw point as their subgroup generator; the `p=593` row instead uses `2 • (2,2)` and needs a multiplier-aware specialization. Lean generator-order certificates remain open for all eight rows. `lake build ConcreteCurveRecovery` completed successfully (9,051 jobs); all four declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`.

## Exact full-population relation count

A frozen post-analysis enumerated every admissible ordered offset pair at the eight subgroup orders. The existing checked Lean declarations `XedniCoefficientFamily.admissible_hit_count` and `distinct_directions_force_zero` give the corresponding general argument: each vector contributes exactly `q−1` admissible pairs; two distinct coefficient directions can meet only at the excluded pair `(w,w)`. All 13 selected vectors have nonzero `a`, `b`, and `a+b` modulo every panel order. Their 78 pairwise determinants have absolute values 1–60, below the smallest subgroup order 293, so the hit sets are disjoint. Thus the full population contains exactly `13(q−1)` relation events among `(q−1)(q−2)` admissible pairs, a fraction `13/(q−2)`.

The direct Lean theorem `XedniPublicFirstLargeHoldout.family_event_count` in `formal/FamilyCount.lean` counts indexed relation events for **any** finite coefficient family over a finite field: if every vector has nonzero first and second coefficients and nonzero coefficient sum, the count is `|family| (|F|−1)`. It invokes the checked `XedniCoefficientFamily.admissible_hit_count` from the pinned, byte-identical `formal/CoefficientFamily.lean`. The separately checked `distinct_directions_force_zero` lemma supplies disjointness when pairwise determinants are nonzero; the panel's determinant and coefficient premises are checked in `formal/Panel.lean` and independently by `post.py` and `post_audit.py`. This proof map distinguishes the universal event theorem from the finite panel certification. The readable proof is the line parametrization by the first offset: each `t₁ ≠ w` determines exactly one `t₂`, and nonzero `a`, `b`, `a+b` ensure both queried points are nonidentity and distinct. Summing the `q−1` points on each line gives `13(q−1)`; disjoint directions turn that event count into the same number of distinct hit pairs.

`formal/AdmissiblePairs.lean` proves `admissible_pair_count`: the whole ordered query space with both sampled points nonidentity and distinct has exactly `(q−1)(q−2)` pairs. It identifies that space with the off-diagonal of the field after removing the target offset. The checked `family_density_cross_mul` theorem combines this denominator with the distinct-hit theorem as `|hits|(q−2)=|family||admissible pairs|`, retaining the explicit nonzero coefficient and pairwise-determinant hypotheses. For the eight checked panel orders, `q−2>0` and `|family|=13`, so the exact fraction is `13/(q−2)`. `lake build AdmissiblePairs` completed successfully (9,043 jobs), and both new declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`.

The same module now states the fraction itself in `family_exact_fraction`: when `q≥3` and an admissible ordered pair is selected uniformly, `|hits|/|admissible pairs|=|family|/(q−2)`. Its `family_fraction_bound` gives `|hits|/|admissible pairs|≤|family|/N` whenever `N>0` and `N≤q−2`. For a fixed family size, the bound tends to zero as the field size grows along instances satisfying the stated coefficient and determinant hypotheses. This is a per-query, fixed-family statement; it does not price the work of selecting coefficients or making repeated adaptive queries. Both declarations passed the pinned 9,043-job build and report only the three standard Lean foundations under `#print axioms`.

The overlap-tolerant result in `formal/FamilyBound.lean` removes the pairwise-determinant hypothesis. `family_hit_card_le` maps indexed relation events onto distinct hit pairs and proves `|hits|≤m(q−1)` for any fixed family of `m` vectors with nonzero first and second coefficients and nonzero sums. `family_fraction_le` gives the uniform admissible-query bound `|hits|/|admissible pairs|≤m/(q−2)` for `q≥3`. The converse `family_size_needed` proves that a target one-query hit fraction at least `δ` requires `m≥δ(q−2)`, regardless of overlapping directions. This is a quantified coefficient-budget obstruction for the fixed-family mechanism; vector selection based on the target and repeated adaptive queries have separate cost and information laws. `lake build FamilyBound` completed successfully (9,044 jobs), and all three declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`.

`formal/BoxBound.lean` makes the coefficient budget geometric. It defines the full set of valid integer coefficient pairs in `[-H,H]²`, proves its size is at most `(2H+1)²`, and applies the overlap-tolerant theorem after reducing coefficients into the field. The checked `bounded_vector_fraction_le` gives uniform one-query hit fraction at most `(2H+1)²/(q−2)`; `coefficient_height_needed` proves that achieving fraction at least `δ` with this box requires `δ(q−2)≤(2H+1)²`. Thus a fixed positive one-query fraction along increasing `q` requires coefficient height on the order of at least `√q` for this exhaustive bounded-box mechanism. This does not count the effort to identify a useful direction from public points or support target-dependent/adaptive selection. `lake build BoxBound` completed successfully (9,045 jobs); all four declarations report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` under `#print axioms`.
