# Exact quarter-shift evaluator on cyclic 432-subgroups

The two maps induced by \(\tau\mapsto\tau\pm1/4\) can be evaluated directly from a cyclic subgroup of order 432. The evaluator uses a degree-four quotient, its dual kernel, and an oriented Weil pairing. Checked Sage runs over \(\mathbf F_{877}\) and \(\mathbf F_{883^2}\) produced both level-27 moduli points. Changing the input generator preserved each output, while Frobenius over \(\mathbf F_{883}\) exchanged the two outputs.

## Construction and proof

Represent a noncuspidal point of \(X_0(432)\) by \((E,C)\), with \(C=\langle P\rangle\) of order 432. Form

\[
K=\langle108P\rangle,\qquad q:E\longrightarrow E_0=E/K,\qquad S=q(P),\qquad R=27S.
\]

Here \(S\) has order 108 and \(R\) has order four. Let \(\widehat q\) be the dual four-isogeny and choose a generator \(T\) of \(\ker\widehat q\). After adjoining a primitive fourth root \(i\), orient \(T\) by \(e_4(R,T)=i\). The output pairs are

\[
\Theta_\pm(E,C)=\bigl(E_0/\langle T\pm R\rangle,
                 \langle\phi_\pm(4S)\rangle\bigr),
\]

where \(\phi_\pm:E_0\to E_0/\langle T\pm R\rangle\) is the four-isogeny. Each indicated subgroup has order 27. The ordered labels depend on the choice of primitive root and Weil-pairing convention; the unordered pair is intrinsic over the ground field. The analytic sign relative to the earlier q-series convention still needs a direct calibration.

For the standard complex point \(E_\tau=\mathbf C/(\mathbf Z+\mathbf Z\tau)\), \(P=1/432\), scale the first quotient lattice to \(L_0=\langle1,4\tau\rangle\). Then \(S=1/108\), \(R=1/4\), and the dual kernel is generated by \(T=\tau\). Consequently

\[
\langle L_0,T\pm R\rangle
=\langle1,4\tau,\tau\pm1/4\rangle
=\langle1,\tau\pm1/4\rangle.
\]

The level-27 subgroup comes from \(4S=1/27\), as required. For a replacement generator \(P'=uP\), \(u\in(\mathbf Z/432)^\times\), the oriented dual generator changes by \(u\bmod4\), and the generated four-kernel is unchanged. This gives a map on the moduli point rather than on its chosen generator. Frobenius at \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) sends \(i\) to \(-i\) and exchanges the oriented kernels.

Sage's direct `q.dual()` raised an internal composite-isogeny error on these degree-four examples, so the producer enumerates the dual kernel and verifies the composite on \(P\). `verify_lattice.py` independently checked 2,662 rational lattice equalities, all 144 possible unit classes modulo 432, and both finite-field receipts. The files `protocol.json`, `sage_runtime.json`, `result.json`, and `validation.json` preserve the inputs and outputs. These are exact correctness checks, with no wall-time comparison.
