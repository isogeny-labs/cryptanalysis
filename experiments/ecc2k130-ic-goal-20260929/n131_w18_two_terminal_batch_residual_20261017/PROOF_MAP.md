The readable argument for the reverse step is commutativity and associativity: if `I·(P·d)=1`, then `(I·P)·d=1`, so `I·P` is the inverse of `d`; also `(I·d)·P=1`, so `I·d` becomes the inverse of the preceding prefix. Starting from one inverse of the full product and applying this identity from the final factor to the first yields every input inverse. Zero denominators use the existing exceptional point-addition branch and are excluded from the batch product; the two frozen 2,080-row panels have zero such cases. A faithful Lean theorem for the complete loop and concrete group map remains open.
