# A target-conditioned norm fills its coefficient rectangle

**Sixteen admissible second-product values give a reduced N41 eliminant with 32,768 nonzero coefficients in a 32,896-position rectangle (99.61% occupancy) and full 128-column separation rank.** This is an exact measurement for the frozen public witness suffix and the frozen negative suffix; the rank is measured on the witness panel. The first eight values fill 12,449 of 16,512 positions (75.39%); repeating that prefix in a separately frozen 16-value run reproduces both counts. An independent pure-Python `GF(2^41)` evaluator checks all 18 saved prefix-product values. A second implementation replays the full matrix rank from its serialized coefficients.

This tests a concrete way to batch the second and third source families. For the interpolated target-conditioned eliminant `F(p,q,r;t)` from the [prior experiment](../n41_first_product_interpolated_eliminant_20261017/RESULT.md), fix a prefix of source-valid second products `q₁,…,qₖ` and compute

`Nₖ(p,r) = (∏ᵢ F(p,qᵢ,r;t)) mod L_R(r)`,

where `L_R` is the monic degree-128 linearized annihilator of the third product's seven-dimensional binary span. Starting with `L₀(X)=X`, each independent basis value `v` updates it by `Lnew(X)=Lold(X)²+Lold(v)Lold(X)`. In characteristic two this kills the old span and `v`; its degree doubles and the root bound shows that these are exactly its roots. For any source-valid `r`, `L_R(r)=0`, hence `Nₖ(p,r)=∏ᵢ F(p,qᵢ,r;t)`. A zero of any included factor is therefore retained by the reduced norm. The prefix starts with the archived witness product `1383481007416` and then uses the smallest distinct admissible products. Its full ordered list is in [protocol_v2.json](protocol_v2.json). The public first and third products are `97508` and `216004441708`.

The witness and negative panels have identical coefficient counts at every checkpoint, while their evaluations differ. The v2 run's peak self RSS was **488,816,640 B**, below the predeclared 4-GiB observational boundary; each panel used one checked Sage process and a 300-second alarm. Each checkpoint records the time for its *last* multiplication and modular reduction in [result_v2.json](result_v2.json). These intervals exclude the preceding products, specialization, initialization, and direct-value checks; they are stage diagnostics, not a whole-query timing. The [runtime receipt](sage_runtime_info.json) was saved before measurement. The [v1 eight-value run](result.json) and its source/protocol remain frozen. Failed first attempts due to an encoder-method mismatch and a prefix-length assertion are retained in `attempt1.stderr` and `attempt_v2a.stderr`.

The independent [verification](verification.json) evaluates the archived 213-term tensor directly with a separate shift-and-XOR field multiplier, at all saved prefixes of both panels. It also checks source and protocol hashes. The witness product evaluates to zero for every prefix; the negative product evaluates nonzero for every prefix. The prior experiment independently checks the seven source pair states and the public curve relation represented by the witness triple.

The coefficient array has rows for `p⁰` through `p²⁵⁶` and columns for `r⁰` through `r¹²⁷`. A separately frozen [rank probe](result_v4.json) serialized all 32,896 field coefficients into a 263,168-byte [matrix](coefficient_matrix_v4.bin). Its Sage rank is 128, and an independent pure-Python [Gaussian elimination receipt](rank_verification.json) obtains the same rank and coefficient occupancy; its first 128 rows already form a nonsingular square submatrix. Any representation of this *polynomial* as `Σᵢ Aᵢ(p)Bᵢ(r)` needs at least 128 terms: the coefficient matrix of each separated term is an outer product of rank at most one. This bound applies to linear separation over `GF(2^41)`. The original product of 16 factors remains a compact arithmetic circuit, so the rank does not bound general circuit size.

The N131 W20 source has **132,045** admissible second-product codes and a 19-dimensional third-product span. If the N41 degree growth and near-full occupancy were to persist when this particular *expanded coefficient-array representation* is extended to all N131 second products, its rectangle would have `(16·132045+1)·2¹⁹ = 1,107,674,267,648` field-element positions. At the minimum 17 bytes per `GF(2^131)` element, that is **17.13 TiB** before indexing or arithmetic overhead. This is a conditional size projection, not an N131 measurement or a lower bound on factored circuits. It makes direct expansion a poor next implementation under the goal's 4-GiB cap. A factored or black-box form could still be useful if it supports root finding without materializing this array.

The next discriminating experiment is a black-box root-finding test that keeps the 16-factor circuit intact and asks whether it rejects whole source-product rectangles without enumerating their members. The [proof map](PROOF_MAP.md) separates the checked abstract reduction lemma from the finite field computation and the remaining polynomial/curve bridges. `candidate_id` remains `null` because this is one component of a target-conditioned point-decomposition solver with four suffix pair states fixed by the archived N41 relation.
