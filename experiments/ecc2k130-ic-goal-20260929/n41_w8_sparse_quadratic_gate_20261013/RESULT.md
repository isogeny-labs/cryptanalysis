# First sparse quadratic census identifies the trace-zero coordinate

The first frozen N41 census evaluated 862 squarefree monomials of degree at most two on the witness-centered width-16 two-pair partial families and eight sampled regions. It found 41 individually zero monomials on every nonempty side. Direct field-basis inspection found `Tr(1)=1` and `Tr(t^i)=0` for `1≤i≤40`, so the reciprocal-coordinate trace-zero law sets bit zero to zero; the 41 zero monomials are that coordinate and its 40 products. These identities supply no additional family pruning. The source and result remain frozen; the version-two census removes the universally zero bit before searching for low-support relations.

The first plain `python3 -B run.py` attempt stopped at import because that interpreter lacked `psutil`; `first_launch_failure.txt` records the failure. The checked repository Sage launcher then ran the same frozen runner. Its runtime receipt is `sage_runtime_info.json`. The one-worker native census completed in 7.744 s with sampled combined RSS 36,159,488 bytes under the 120 s / 4 GiB cap. It preserved the witness S3 coordinate hit, and the result contains the eight sampled regions, including one empty region omitted from the evaluated cases.
