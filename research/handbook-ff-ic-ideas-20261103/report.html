Handbook of Finite Fields → 100 index-calculus ideas for ECDLP (2026-11-03)

Question. Which mechanisms in the Handbook of Finite Fields (Mullen and Panario, eds., CRC 2013; table of contents, chapters 5–17, photographed by the owner) could lower the cost of one phase of an elliptic-curve index calculus, or open a non-generic route, and which of them has this lab already tried?

Status. Catalogue only. Every entry is a proposal with a falsifiable claim, a preregistered prediction, the cheapest discriminating test and a falsifier. Nothing here is measured, verified or an improvement; under the success criterion (AGENTS.md) an idea becomes a success only when a frozen, replayed measurement lowers cost against the named IC baseline.

Method

  1. Mine (14 Sonnet agents, one per ToC page). Each transcribed the section numbers it could read and proposed 8–10 mechanism-specific ideas tied to a section, with the lab’s already-integrated techniques named as off limits.
  2. Prior art (14 Haiku agents). Each idea was grepped against the lab’s IC records listed in prior-art-index.md and classified untried, partially-tried, tried-open or tried-closed with paths. The cross-repository lead atlas (crypto-autoresearcher) and the handbook knowledge base catalogue (knowledge/finite-fields/kb, catalogue not published in this checkout) were not reachable; both remain open checks.
  3. Select (1 Sonnet agent, structured output validated to exactly 100 distinct ids). Duplicates merged, relabellings and closed leads dropped, phases balanced, ids H001..H100 grouped by phase.
  4. Formalize (10 Sonnet agents, 10 ideas each) wrote formal/HandbookIC/Ideas/Bkk.lean: for each idea a def Hnnn_claim : Prop with every curve-specific fact as an explicit hypothesis, plus at least one proved supporting theorem. Verify (Haiku per batch): compile, forbidden tokens, #print axioms, claim declarations present; one Sonnet repair round for failures, then re-verification.
  5. Critic (1 Sonnet agent): coverage, relabelling, duplicates, weak falsifiers, top ten, missing angles.
  6. Native evidence: formal/check.sh (full lake build, token scan, axiom audit, receipts/) and the CI gate tools/lean/current-corpus-gate.py, extended in this change to admit registered dependency-free core-Lean projects (CORE_LEAN_PROJECTS), with refusal controls in tools/lean/test_current_corpus_gate.py.

Formalization scope (partial, by design)

The Lean corpus models the exponent side only: a DLPInstance is a type with a log bijection onto Fin n; factor bases, relations, smoothness counts, orbit quotients and phase costs are abstract. The elliptic-curve group law, field arithmetic, summation polynomials and character sums are not modelled, so each Hnnn_claim carries those facts as hypotheses and each record names its missing_bridge. The theorems are cost-model and counting lemmas (no sorry, standard foundations only). Mathlib was not used: the session’s disk allowance could not hold the pinned Mathlib cache, and the lab’s Mathlib-pinned Hecke workspace is the route for any idea that advances to a measured result. This is the “partial formalization” case of the Lean rule in AGENTS.md: the full elliptic-curve statements stay open.

What the critic found (adversarial pass, kept verbatim in critic.json)

The catalogue has 100 ideas, and three things limit it. First, coverage is lopsided: decomposition (25) and factor-base (18) hold 43 of the 100, linear-algebra has 4 (2 of them Spot logistics), and descent has 7. Second, many ideas measure a distribution or predictor rather than a changed algorithm: about 12 are best-of-T subspace or coset selection (H001,H004,H008,H009,H010,H013,H014,H015,H017,H042,H072,H073). A13 already measured coset selection as near-null, and their own predictions shrink to about 1.0x at production size. Third, about 8 are closed or admitted-null re-asks (H002,H020,H078,H083,H088,H089,H100,H065). I flagged 18 relabels, 27 duplicates (several of them chains, such as H015, H044, H024/H026 and H097/H099), and 36 weak ideas. Relabel examples are H050, H090, H086, H084, H076, H062, H064/H093, H095, H077. The strongest first-improvement candidates change something countable and have a checkable output: group-structure quotients (H044, H007), early stopping (H045), identical-output hot-loop rewrites (H026, H097, H098), the cofactor factor base (H012), core extraction before Wiedemann (H059), a 33% matvec cut (H066), and isotropic F4 pairs (H041).   • Missing angles named by the critic:
    • Linear-algebra is thin (4 ideas, 2 of them Spot logistics H064/H093). There is nothing on Pohlig-Hellman/CRT interplay with small prime factors of the group order, lower-precision modular arithmetic, structured elimination before Lanczos, or LA for the m=2 low-weight rows.
    • Decomposition and factor-base hold 43 of 100 ideas, and roughly 12 of them are variants of best-of-T subspace or coset selection (H001,H004,H008,H009,H010,H013,H014,H015,H017,H042,H072,H073). A13 already measured coset selection as near-null. This is oversupplied and unlikely to produce a first improvement.
    • Prime-field curves (ECCp-131, Curve25519-class) are covered only by H017, H044, H054, H087 and H097. The Weil-restriction, F4, and subspace ideas are binary-specific, and no idea covers prime-field decomposition (for example Gaudry/Diem-style degree-2 and index-calculus-for-prime-field-extension).
    • The large-prime / double-large-prime relation cost model has no idea, although large-prime variants are claimed integrated. There is no model for how the large-prime cut interacts with orbit quotients (H062, H057 only handle the full-rank threshold).
    • Nearly every cheapest_test is on n=17-31 toys while targets are n=53..131. Only H090/H097/H098/H099 touch n=53+. No idea has a scaling-extrapolation test (a fit over n with a prediction interval at n=131), so toy wins cannot be turned into a distance to rho.
    • No idea reports the matched-rho gap or prices unpriced native work at 0/0.1/1 GAE, apart from H049, H090 and H076. Per section 5a, each should name its rho reference.
    • Hybrid guess strategy for Gröbner solving (how many variables to guess, with the bilinear structure) has only H019/H022/H071. Nothing covers dedicated solvers, such as SAT or XL with the Semaev structure over GF(2) and Frobenius-twisted equations, or specialised F4 linear algebra (H030 only touches the backend).
    • Handbook areas underused: Chapter 8-style zeta/L-function and point-count machinery beyond Weil bounds, Chapter 11.4 factorization (only H034), Chapter 13.4 sparse structured methods (used for PLE only), isogeny/endomorphism ideas outside j=0 (H087 only), and Chapters 16-17 coding/quantum (H091 aside). The Kloosterman/Walsh family (H001,H039,H048) is reused repeatedly with little diversity.
    • Curve-selection has six ideas, three are closed or null (H088,H089,H086), and none addresses GLV/GLS-compatible order policy or composite-order cofactor choice.
    • Cross-phase composition is missing: no idea tests that two accepted gains (for example the H007 iota quotient plus the H044 order-6 quotient, or the H045 stop rule plus the H059 core) compose multiplicatively on the same session.

Claim limits

  • Every idea is a proposal. The predictions are the authors’ preregistered numbers and were not checked against any run. No cost, speedup or rho gap is reported because nothing was measured.
  • The prior-art statuses come from a grep over local records by Haiku agents, confirmed by opening at most two files per idea; the atlas match and the handbook knowledge-base catalogue are open checks. A tried-closed idea kept in the catalogue is kept because the selector judged the handbook angle new; the critic disputes several (H002, H020, H065, H078, H083, H088, H089, H100).
  • The Lean theorems are supporting lemmas about abstract definitions; each record’s missing_bridge names the curve or field fact its Prop assumes. No theorem models the elliptic-curve group law, and a stated Hnnn_claim is not evidence that it holds.
  • The ToC photographs were transcribed by the miners (pages.json); section numbers may carry reading errors from rotated pages and should be checked against the book before citation.

factor-base (18)

H001 — Kloosterman/Walsh ranking of affine cosets for the factor base

  • Handbook: 17.2.1 Mutually unbiased bases · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H001_claim; theorems: H001_partition_sum, H001_no_gain_without_variance)
  • Claim: Valid-point counts across the 2^(n-m) cosets of a fixed V exceed binomial spread; a dual-sum (Walsh) predictor ranks cosets with Spearman >= 0.6, so best-of-K gives >= 1.05x relations per factor-base element. A13 measured coset choice as near-null, so this is a predictor test only.
  • Prediction: Spread 1.0-1.5x binomial SD; predictor rho 0.6-0.9; best-of-64 gain 1.03-1.10x, shrinking with n.
  • Cheapest test: n=17, m=8: all 512 cosets; count valid points and S3 relations over 2000 targets; correlate with Walsh prediction; 5 random b.
  • Falsifier: Spearman < 0.3, or best-of-64 gain < 1.02x with bootstrap CI containing 1.
  • Lean gap (assumed, not proved): Walsh/Kloosterman dual-sum predictor correlation (Spearman >= 0.6) with coset valid-point counts and the resulting best-of-64 gain; claim quantifies over observation records.

H002 — Cyclic-shift window factor base: Frobenius merge factor about 2

  • Handbook: 17.1.6.3 Linear polynomial dynamical systems · Prior art: tried-closed · Lean: proved (H002_claim; theorems: H002_stay_count, H002_claim_holds)
  • Claim: New angle only on the closed census: for a window V=span{x,…,x(2(m-1))} of dim about n/2, Frobenius classes number |V|/c with c in [1.8,3] independent of n, so orbit quotient shrinks columns about 2x, not n x. The existence obstruction itself is closed.
  • Prediction: Mean class size about 3.0; classes/|V| about 0.5 (0.33-0.56); LA B^2 term falls at most about 4x.
  • Cheapest test: n=19,29 (also 11,13,37): enumerate window elements, compute classes by repeated squaring, report class-size distribution.
  • Falsifier: Column reduction >= 4 for the window at n=29 or 37.
  • Lean gap (assumed, not proved): That Frobenius class sizes of the window span{x,…,x(2(m-1))} in GF(2^n) are at most 3 and sum to |V| (hypothesis of the claim); the shift action as a bit shift on window coordinates.

H003 — Kneser/Freiman-extremal subspaces with small product span

  • Handbook: 16.7.2 Polynomial multiplication, 16.7.3 Matrix multiplication structure · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H003_claim; theorems: H003_ap_sums, H003_claim_holds, H003_ap_le_generic)
  • Claim: Among d-dim subspaces of GF(2^n), n prime, dim(VV)=2d-1 holds only for uspan{1,w,..,w^(d-1)}; random subspaces give min(n,d(d+1)/2). Product-span size controls the linearised-decision candidate dimension.
  • Prediction: n=23,d=7: random V gives dim 23; interval V gives 13; no non-AP subspace in 1000 samples drops below 2d-1.
  • Cheapest test: n=17..23, d=5..8: sample subspaces, Gaussian-eliminate pairwise products, histogram dim(V*V).
  • Falsifier: A non-AP subspace with dim(V*V)<2d-1 at prime n, or random subspaces below the generic value at >1%.
  • Lean gap (assumed, not proved): Kneser/Freiman lower bound dim(VV) >= 2d-1 and the equality characterisation (V is uspan{1,w,..,w^(d-1)}) in GF(2^n), n prime; both are hypotheses. The AP sumset count is proved on exponents only.

H004 — Fano-factor selection of equal-size subspace factor bases

  • Falsifier: Dispersion within 0.9-1.1 of null, or best-of-M gain <1.05 after multiple-comparison correction.
  • Lean gap (assumed, not proved): Over-dispersion of the solution count N(V) over random k-dim subspaces of GF(2^n) (Fano factor vs symmetric null) and the measured best-of-M gain >= 1.2x; proved part is only the cost arithmetic.

H005 — Trace-code rank-profile criterion for factor-base subspace shape

  • Handbook: 15.1.2.4 Subfield and trace codes, 15.1.2.3 Reed-Muller codes · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H005_claim; theorems: H005_sum_counts, H005_best_ge)

H006 — Cyclotomic-coset factor base x^s in a Frobenius orbit

H007 — 2-torsion translation as extra projectivity in subfield orbit quotient

  • Handbook: 14.4.2 Collineations and coordinate frames · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H007_claim; theorems: H007_monotone, H007_half, H007_claim_holds, H007_cost_improves)

H008 — Exponential-sum bias of S3 triple count T(V) across subspaces

  • Handbook: 12.5.3 Bounds for rational places, 12.7.3 Case of curves · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H008_claim; theorems: H008_le_cube, H008_cost_antitone, H008_perm_invariant)

H009 — Artin-Schreier splitting density of cosets of V

  • Handbook: 12.1.3 Extensions of function fields · Prior art: partially-tried · Lean: proved (H009_claim; theorems: H009_tuples_length, H009_good_count, H009_monotone)
  • Claim: Only about half of V lies on E (Tr(v+a+b/v^2)=0); the best coset has density 0.5+O(sqrt(k(n-k))2^(-k/2)), raising useful fraction by (rho/0.5)^m, visible at small k and vanishing at cryptographic size. Koblitz trace-zero policy already forces density 1.
  • Prediction: n=24,k=8,m=3: best of 10^4 cosets rho 0.60-0.70, gain 1.7-2.7x; n=48,k=16 gain <1.15x.
  • Cheapest test: n=20..30: E-density of 10^4 random cosets; fit best density to Chernoff prediction; S3 solves on best vs median V.
  • Falsifier: Best-of-10^4 density within 0.02 of 0.5 at n=24,k=8, or gain <1.3x useful relations per call.
  • Lean gap (assumed, not proved): Artin-Schreier splitting criterion Tr(v+a+b/v^2)=0 defining the on-curve predicate, the Chernoff best-coset density 0.5+O(sqrt(k(n-k))2^(-k/2)), and Koblitz trace-zero policy; the strict-improvement conjunct of the claim is stated but not proved.

H010 — Legendre-sequence bias selection of affine cosets (odd p and binary)
