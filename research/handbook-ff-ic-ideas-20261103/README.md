# Handbook of Finite Fields → 100 index-calculus ideas for ECDLP (2026-11-03)

**Question.** Which mechanisms in the *Handbook of Finite Fields* (Mullen and
Panario, eds., CRC 2013; table of contents, chapters 5–17, photographed by
the owner) could lower the cost of one phase of an elliptic-curve index
calculus, or open a non-generic route, and which of them has this lab
already tried?

**Status.** Catalogue only. Every entry is a *proposal* with a falsifiable
claim, a preregistered prediction, the cheapest discriminating test and a
falsifier. Nothing here is measured, verified or an improvement; under the
success criterion (AGENTS.md) an idea becomes a success only when a frozen,
replayed measurement lowers cost against the named IC baseline.

## Method

1. **Mine** (14 Sonnet agents, one per ToC page). Each transcribed the
   section numbers it could read and proposed 8–10 mechanism-specific ideas
   tied to a section, with the lab's already-integrated techniques named as
   off limits.
2. **Prior art** (14 Haiku agents). Each idea was grepped against the lab's
   IC records listed in `prior-art-index.md` and classified `untried`,
   `partially-tried`, `tried-open` or `tried-closed` with paths. The
   cross-repository lead atlas (crypto-autoresearcher) and the handbook
   knowledge base catalogue (`knowledge/finite-fields/kb`, catalogue not
   published in this checkout) were not reachable; both remain open checks.
3. **Select** (1 Sonnet agent, structured output validated to exactly 100
   distinct ids). Duplicates merged, relabellings and closed leads dropped,
   phases balanced, ids `H001..H100` grouped by phase.
4. **Formalize** (10 Sonnet agents, 10 ideas each) wrote
   `formal/HandbookIC/Ideas/Bkk.lean`: for each idea a `def Hnnn_claim : Prop`
   with every curve-specific fact as an explicit hypothesis, plus at least one
   proved supporting theorem. **Verify** (Haiku per batch): compile, forbidden
   tokens, `#print axioms`, claim declarations present; one Sonnet repair
   round for failures, then re-verification.
5. **Critic** (1 Sonnet agent): coverage, relabelling, duplicates, weak
   falsifiers, top ten, missing angles.
6. Native evidence: `formal/check.sh` (full `lake build`, token scan, axiom
   audit, `receipts/`) and the CI gate `tools/lean/current-corpus-gate.py`,
   extended in this change to admit registered dependency-free core-Lean
   projects (`CORE_LEAN_PROJECTS`), with refusal controls in
   `tools/lean/test_current_corpus_gate.py`.

## Formalization scope (partial, by design)

The Lean corpus models the exponent side only: a `DLPInstance` is a type with
a log bijection onto `Fin n`; factor bases, relations, smoothness counts,
orbit quotients and phase costs are abstract. The elliptic-curve group law,
field arithmetic, summation polynomials and character sums are **not**
modelled, so each `Hnnn_claim` carries those facts as hypotheses and each
record names its `missing_bridge`. The theorems are cost-model and counting
lemmas (no `sorry`, standard foundations only). Mathlib was not used: the
session's disk allowance could not hold the pinned Mathlib cache, and the
lab's Mathlib-pinned Hecke workspace is the route for any idea that advances
to a measured result. This is the "partial formalization" case of the Lean
rule in AGENTS.md: the full elliptic-curve statements stay open.

### What the critic found (adversarial pass, kept verbatim in `critic.json`)

The catalogue has 100 ideas, and three things limit it. First, coverage is lopsided: decomposition (25) and factor-base (18) hold 43 of the 100, linear-algebra has 4 (2 of them Spot logistics), and descent has 7. Second, many ideas measure a distribution or predictor rather than a changed algorithm: about 12 are best-of-T subspace or coset selection (H001,H004,H008,H009,H010,H013,H014,H015,H017,H042,H072,H073). A13 already measured coset selection as near-null, and their own predictions shrink to about 1.0x at production size. Third, about 8 are closed or admitted-null re-asks (H002,H020,H078,H083,H088,H089,H100,H065). I flagged 18 relabels, 27 duplicates (several of them chains, such as H015, H044, H024/H026 and H097/H099), and 36 weak ideas. Relabel examples are H050, H090, H086, H084, H076, H062, H064/H093, H095, H077. The strongest first-improvement candidates change something countable and have a checkable output: group-structure quotients (H044, H007), early stopping (H045), identical-output hot-loop rewrites (H026, H097, H098), the cofactor factor base (H012), core extraction before Wiedemann (H059), a 33% matvec cut (H066), and isotropic F4 pairs (H041). Merge the duplicate chains before spending runs. Add LA, prime-field, large-prime accounting and scale-extrapolation ideas. Every winner must report the gap to the matched rho with the section 5a pricing band.

- Relabelling flags (18): H050, H090, H086, H084, H076, H062, H074, H095, H064, H093, H077, H058, H083, H075, H078, H009, H063, H048. These re-account or rename a cost rather than change the algorithm; under the success criterion they cannot be improvements and stay only as accounting checks.
- Duplicate flags (27): H092→H063, H048→H039, H014→H004, H042→H013, H008→H004, H010→H015, H009→H015, H001→H015, H073→H015, H072→H013, H017→H015, H054→H044, H087→H044, H031→H023, H026→H024, H049→H045, H059→H057, H058→H057, H053→H046, H099→H097, H043→H025, H100→H018, H082→H080, H041→H038, H029→H075, H066→H065, H002→H088. A later pass should merge these chains before any is scheduled.
- Weak-falsifier flags (37): H020, H100, H065, H009, H010, H015, H017, H073, H072, H001, H002, H078, H083, H088, H089, H077, H080, H082, H079, H064, H093, H095, H091, H094, H096, H081, H068, H069, H070, H011, H021, H067, H004, H014, H051, H085, H074.
- Missing angles named by the critic:
  - Linear-algebra is thin (4 ideas, 2 of them Spot logistics H064/H093). There is nothing on Pohlig-Hellman/CRT interplay with small prime factors of the group order, lower-precision modular arithmetic, structured elimination before Lanczos, or LA for the m=2 low-weight rows.
  - Decomposition and factor-base hold 43 of 100 ideas, and roughly 12 of them are variants of best-of-T subspace or coset selection (H001,H004,H008,H009,H010,H013,H014,H015,H017,H042,H072,H073). A13 already measured coset selection as near-null. This is oversupplied and unlikely to produce a first improvement.
  - Prime-field curves (ECCp-131, Curve25519-class) are covered only by H017, H044, H054, H087 and H097. The Weil-restriction, F4, and subspace ideas are binary-specific, and no idea covers prime-field decomposition (for example Gaudry/Diem-style degree-2 and index-calculus-for-prime-field-extension).
  - The large-prime / double-large-prime relation cost model has no idea, although large-prime variants are claimed integrated. There is no model for how the large-prime cut interacts with orbit quotients (H062, H057 only handle the full-rank threshold).
  - Nearly every cheapest_test is on n=17-31 toys while targets are n=53..131. Only H090/H097/H098/H099 touch n=53+. No idea has a scaling-extrapolation test (a fit over n with a prediction interval at n=131), so toy wins cannot be turned into a distance to rho.
  - No idea reports the matched-rho gap or prices unpriced native work at 0/0.1/1 GAE, apart from H049, H090 and H076. Per section 5a, each should name its rho reference.
  - Hybrid guess strategy for Gröbner solving (how many variables to guess, with the bilinear structure) has only H019/H022/H071. Nothing covers dedicated solvers, such as SAT or XL with the Semaev structure over GF(2) and Frobenius-twisted equations, or specialised F4 linear algebra (H030 only touches the backend).
  - Handbook areas underused: Chapter 8-style zeta/L-function and point-count machinery beyond Weil bounds, Chapter 11.4 factorization (only H034), Chapter 13.4 sparse structured methods (used for PLE only), isogeny/endomorphism ideas outside j=0 (H087 only), and Chapters 16-17 coding/quantum (H091 aside). The Kloosterman/Walsh family (H001,H039,H048) is reused repeatedly with little diversity.
  - Curve-selection has six ideas, three are closed or null (H088,H089,H086), and none addresses GLV/GLS-compatible order policy or composite-order cofactor choice.
  - Cross-phase composition is missing: no idea tests that two accepted gains (for example the H007 iota quotient plus the H044 order-6 quotient, or the H045 stop rule plus the H059 core) compose multiplicatively on the same session.

### Claim limits

- Every idea is a proposal. The predictions are the authors' preregistered numbers and were not checked against any run. No cost, speedup or rho gap is reported because nothing was measured.
- The prior-art statuses come from a grep over local records by Haiku agents, confirmed by opening at most two files per idea; the atlas match and the handbook knowledge-base catalogue are open checks. A `tried-closed` idea kept in the catalogue is kept because the selector judged the handbook angle new; the critic disputes several (H002, H020, H065, H078, H083, H088, H089, H100).
- The Lean theorems are supporting lemmas about abstract definitions; each record's `missing_bridge` names the curve or field fact its `Prop` assumes. No theorem models the elliptic-curve group law, and a stated `Hnnn_claim` is not evidence that it holds.
- The ToC photographs were transcribed by the miners (`pages.json`); section numbers may carry reading errors from rotated pages and should be checked against the book before citation.
