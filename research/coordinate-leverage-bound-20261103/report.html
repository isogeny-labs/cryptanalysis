Coordinate-system leverage bound for the recorded IC rows (2026-11-03)

Can a curve equation or coordinate system bring a recorded IC row to parity with rho? (2026-11-03)

Kind. Derived record. No new run. Every input number is copied from a frozen record and cited by path and line in RESULTS.md and data.json. Verdict. No recorded row reaches parity through a coordinate change alone, under the lab’s own pricing of the alternatives. The closest row misses by 1% under the most generous reading, so the question is not closed by a wide margin and the deciding measurement is named below.

Why this record exists

On 2026-11-03 the owner asked whether the statement “a coordinate win cannot move those rows into parity” had been established. It had not: it was an inference in chat with no data behind it. This record is the derivation, with its assumptions exposed, and the inventory of what the lab has measured about coordinates and curve shapes.

Method

A coordinate system or curve model changes the cost of each elliptic-curve group operation and nothing else: relation yield, factor-base size, linear algebra and descent counts are unchanged. If a fraction f_g of a row’s IC cost is group operations and a coordinate change makes each one c times cheaper, the whole-row speedup is Amdahl’s

speedup(c) = 1 / ((1 - f_g) + f_g / c)

and parity with the recorded gap g needs c* = f_g / (1/g - (1 - f_g)), unreachable when 1/g <= 1 - f_g.

Two inputs are needed per row. The gap is on every frozen row. The share f_g is recorded on one row only (ic_group 315 of ic_total 537 GAE on the a1 n29 Frobenius-orbit arm). For the other rows the record uses the envelope f_g = 1, the assumption most favourable to coordinates, under which c* equals the gap itself.

The available c comes from the lab’s pricing of the alternatives to its shipping arithmetic, which is batched affine on every measured row (the n41 to n61 baselines are even priced in units of one batched affine addition). In ecc2k130/LAMBDA-PROJECTIVE.md the shipping batch-16 affine update costs 38.1 carry-less lane instructions, the batch-infinity limit 36.3, and every projective form more (lambda-projective mixed 60.5, full 78.5). The explicit-formula operation counts in the lab’s EFD import say the same for prime and binary curves: affine addition is 2M 1S 1I against 7M 4S for Jacobian mixed addition or 9M 5S for Lopez-Dahab, and with the inversion batched over k >= 16 points affine stays cheapest. So the realistic factor still available is the batch-depth limit, c = 1.05; the generous reading takes the ALU-count ratio of 1.30 as if a coordinate change could win it rather than lose it.

What the lab has measured about coordinates and shapes

The inventory is in RESULTS.md. In one line each: the only controlled coordinate arm against matched rho is the genus-2 weighted-projective state versus its affine wrapper at 1.10 pooled, both arms 3.8 to 4.2 times behind rho; the P-256 Jacobian seed ladder is 17.4 times faster per seed but is setup only; the ECC2K-130 lambda-projective pricing favours affine by 1.59 and was never built; the Curve25519 Edwards and Montgomery factor-base coordinates give F4 costs within 1% of the Weierstrass frame at k = 20 with no DLP arm. No experiment in either repository has treated the coordinate system of an elliptic-curve IC or rho run as the variable at the DLP level with an A/A arm.

Claim limits

  • Derived, not measured. The envelope f_g = 1 overstates what coordinates can do on eight of nine rows; the true shares are smaller (the n83 row’s dominant cost is an index build, not group operations).
  • The available c is an operation-count bound. Cache behaviour, batching depth and instruction mix on a given host can move it; that is exactly what catalogue idea H097 measures.
  • The gaps are each record’s own unit and reference; they are compared here only through the dimensionless ratio each record states.

The measurement that decides it

H097 in research/handbook-ff-ic-ideas-20261103/IDEAS.md: batch-affine versus projective candidate generation, binary and prime split, 10^7 additions per scheme with matched tables and seeds on isolab-eks, an A/A arm, perf-ipc cycles. Its outcome replaces the c column above with a measured number; the n41 panel row is where that number matters.
