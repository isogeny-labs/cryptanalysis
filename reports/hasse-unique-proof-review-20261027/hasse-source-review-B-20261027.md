## Exact statement and nonvacuity

The theorem universally quantifies six natural numbers `r width n m k l`. Its premises are `width < r`, `n = k*r`, `m = l*r`, `n ≤ m+width`, and `m ≤ n+width`. Its conclusion is `n=m`. It neither names a prime field nor assumes primality, point membership, elliptic group laws, Hasse, or a certificate predicate. There is no assumed `n=m` conclusion. Since `width<r`, `r=0` cannot satisfy its premises. The premises are satisfiable, e.g. `r=7`, `width=3`, `n=m=14`, `k=l=2`. The strict width bound matters: at `width=r=7`, `n=0`, `m=7`, `k=0`, `l=1`, the other premises hold and the conclusion is false. These are source-level boundary observations, not a fresh Lean control run or blind review A's independent result.

The symmetric inequalities express `|n-m|≤width` for natural numbers. Two naturals in a common closed interval whose endpoint difference is at most `width` satisfy them. The README's line 89–92 statement is therefore faithful to this exact conditional arithmetic theorem. The theorem proves uniqueness once the multiples and interval closeness are already established; it does not produce either one.
